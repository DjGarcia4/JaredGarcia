When I redesigned this portfolio, I ran Lighthouse before touching anything so I'd have a baseline. On the home page, mobile performance was **60**. I expected that. What I didn't expect showed up later, when I measured the inner pages: `/projects` and every case study had a **CLS of 0.75**.

For reference, Google considers a CLS under 0.1 "good". At 0.75, the whole page was jumping around in front of visitors, and I had never seen it happen.

## Why I couldn't see it

The site is a Vue SPA with lazy-loaded routes: each page is its own JavaScript chunk, downloaded only when you navigate to it.

```js
{
  path: "/projects",
  name: "projects",
  component: () => import("../views/ProjectsView.vue"),
}
```

On my computer, with the chunk cached and a fast connection, the route shows up almost instantly. On a phone on slow 4G, which is what Lighthouse simulates, a few hundred milliseconds pass between the app starting and the chunk arriving. During that window, the layout looked like this:

1. The `<header>` paints at the top.
2. `<main>` is **empty**: there's nothing to render inside it yet.
3. The `<footer>` sits right under the header, near the top of the screen.
4. The chunk arrives, the page renders, and the footer shoots down.

That last step is the shift. The footer is big (it has a full-width wordmark), so it moves a lot of screen area, which is why the score was so high.

## The fix: reserve the space

The fix was one line: give `<main>` a minimum height of one screen, so the footer starts at the bottom even before the route arrives.

```html
<!-- min-h: while the route chunk loads, <main> is empty; without a
     min height the footer paints at the top and then jumps (CLS). -->
<main id="main" tabindex="-1" class="min-h-[100svh] pt-20">
  <RouterView />
</main>
```

I used `100svh` instead of `100vh` on purpose. On mobile, `vh` measures the screen with the browser bar hidden, while `svh` measures it with the bar visible. With `svh`, the reserved height is never bigger than what's actually on screen.

With that, CLS on `/projects` and the case studies went from **0.75 to 0.001**.

## What the mistake taught me

The most useful part wasn't the fix but understanding why I'd missed it:

- **My setup hid the problem.** The shift only exists while the chunk is in flight. With a warm cache and fiber, that moment is too short to notice.
- **I only measured the home page.** The home page ships in the main bundle, so it didn't have the problem. The lazy routes did. Since then, I run Lighthouse on one page of each type, not just the entry point.
- **Lazy loading has a layout cost.** Splitting JavaScript into chunks is good, but you have to reserve space for whatever hasn't arrived yet. Images without `width` and `height` have the same problem.

## The rest of the performance pass

The CLS was the most striking issue, but not the only one. In the same round:

- **Responsive screenshot variants.** Every project image has 800 px and 1600 px WebP versions, served with `srcset` and `sizes`. The original is kept only for the lightbox. The home page went from 755 KB to 376 KB.
- **A faster hero LCP.** The role and the lead paragraph had an entrance animation that started them at zero opacity. Since they're the largest element on screen on mobile, Lighthouse picks them as the LCP, and the animation delayed it. Now they paint without animation.
- **Initial JavaScript in cacheable pieces.** The initial bundle went from 695 KB gzip in a single file to about 159 KB gzip across four chunks. Vue, GSAP and the icons each get their own chunk, and those barely change between deploys. three.js, which only one case study's 3D demo uses, downloads when you scroll near it.

## The numbers

Lighthouse 12, production build, mobile with default throttling:

| Page | Mobile perf. | Desktop perf. | Accessibility | CLS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home (before) | 60 | 93 | 80 | — |
| Home (after) | 90 | 100 | 100 | — |
| `/projects` (after) | 88 | 99 | 100 | 0.001 |
| Wink App case (after) | 85 | 99 | 100 | 0.001 |

There's still work to do: mobile LCP is still between 3.2 and 3.8 seconds, held back mainly by Google Fonts and by the content being built in the browser. The next steps are self-hosting the fonts and prerendering the routes. When I do, I'll write about it here.
